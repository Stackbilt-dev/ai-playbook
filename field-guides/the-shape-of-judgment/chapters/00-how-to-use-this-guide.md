# 0. How to Use This Guide

Judgment is not a synonym for model output. It is the controlled passage from an uncertain situation to a decision with consequences.

Read Parts I and II in order the first time. They establish the difference between context, claims, patterns, evidence, and evolving state. Then use Part III as an operational review for any workflow that can commit an external effect.

## Use Two Passes

On the first pass, ask whether the system can represent the decision honestly:

- Who is affected?
- Which facts are observed, inferred, forecast, preferred, or required?
- What context could change the meaning of those facts?
- What evidence is missing or conflicting?
- What would cause the system to abstain?

On the second pass, ask whether the system may act:

- Which actor is capable of the next step?
- Which actor has authority for it now?
- Which state transition is being requested?
- What approval or evidence gates protect that transition?
- What durable receipt will show what actually happened?

A fluent answer to the first set does not grant permission in the second.

## Use Artifacts, Not Hidden Reasoning

The guide does not depend on exposing private model reasoning. It asks for compact, inspectable artifacts:

- a context map;
- a claim ledger;
- a decision-state record;
- an evidence gap list;
- a proposed transition;
- an approval or denial record;
- an outcome receipt;
- a promotion or retirement decision.

These can be generated by a model, deterministic code, a person, or a mixture. The system should validate them outside the prose that produced them.

## Scale the Ceremony to Consequence

A reversible content suggestion may need a lightweight claim check. A credit decision, medical recommendation, employment action, or public policy intervention needs domain-specific controls far beyond this guide.

Do not use a detailed worksheet to make an unsafe decision look rigorous. Increase review with consequence, uncertainty, irreversibility, affected population, and difficulty of correction.

## Field Test

Take one recent decision made with AI assistance. Reconstruct its context, claims, evidence, authority, and outcome using the [Decision Record](../worksheets/decision-record.md). Any field you cannot fill is a boundary the original process left implicit.

## Falsifier

If the artifacts do not help a reviewer find missing evidence, explain a route, prevent an unauthorized action, or improve a later decision, simplify or remove them. Inspectability is valuable only when it changes review or behavior.
